## Design Specificity Verdict

### Independent design assessment

Spike has a product-specific foundation. Court geometry appears on sign-in, Home, lineup setup, and live tracking. The scoreboard, player positions, volleyball language, and monospace numbers make the core feel authored for courtside work.

The identity weakens in the two places that matter most. Live scoring becomes a conventional dashboard of eight equal buttons. Post-match Review exposes internal copy such as chart descriptions, compact-row descriptions, and a render-speed target. The result is a distinctive volleyball product whose primary interaction and ending do not yet fulfill its visual idea.

## Overall Impression

Spike looks like a real volleyball tool, not a generic dashboard. Login and Home set a confident tone. The court view is the strongest visual element. The largest opportunity is to make the live mobile interaction as fast and focused as the brand promises.

## Cognitive Load

Six of eight checks fail, which is high cognitive load.

- Live Court lacks a single focus. Score, serve, player, set stats, eight outcomes, Dig, Undo, Substitute, sync, and event history compete.
- Eight outcomes appear in one group, beyond the four-item working-memory guideline.
- Team & Lineup combines team setup, match details, serve choice, lineup assignment, roster creation, editing, bench movement, and match start.
- Live Court exposes 11 immediate controls. The Exit sheet exposes up to six choices. Review exposes five analytics tabs.
- On phone, a coach must remember the selected player while scrolling from the court to the action panel.
- Scoring and lineup complexity appear before the user asks for them.

Grouping and top-level hierarchy pass. Panels organize related content, and scores, the court, and the primary Home action remain clear anchors.

## Emotional Journey

- **Entry:** Sign-in feels confident and focused. The court backdrop and restrained accent establish identity.
- **First use:** Home reassures the user with a clear next step, readiness list, and first-match guide.
- **Setup:** Confidence drops on Team & Lineup because first-time roster creation and match-day setup share one long workspace.
- **Live match:** The scoreboard and court create the peak. Undo and last-action feedback reduce fear. On phone, scrolling between player and action creates urgency and doubt.
- **Match end:** The interface presents an amber warning that the match is final, then exposes implementation-oriented Review copy. The ending feels like an exception state instead of an earned result.

## What's Working

1. **The court is the interface.** Home, Team & Lineup, and Live Court use real volleyball space instead of abstract lists.
2. **Critical state has a visible home.** Score, set, serve, rotation, timeouts, selected player, last action, local save, and cloud sync are represented.
3. **Live Court has a strong accessibility core.** Player tiles have descriptive labels, roving focus, arrow-key movement, Enter/Space activation, and visible focus.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] Mobile live tracking breaks the game-speed loop

**What it is:** Below 980 px, the court and action area form one vertical column. The coach taps a player, scrolls below a square court, and then picks an outcome. Below 680 px, the Game Review switch disappears.

**Why it matters:** A distracted courtside coach can fall behind the rally, forget the selected player, or record the wrong action.

**Fix:** Keep score, selected player, and rally actions inside one viewport. Put a sticky command deck in the phone thumb zone. Keep the court in the upper half. Preserve a visible mobile route to Game Review.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable adapt`

### [P1] The scoring decision is too flat

**What it is:** Kill, Attack Error, Block, Ace, Service Error, Opponent Error, Opponent Winner, and Receive Error appear as eight equal choices during every rally.

**Why it matters:** The coach must scan a large choice set during the app's most time-sensitive task.

**Fix:** Make the first choice “Our point” or “Opponent point,” then reveal the relevant cause. Keep the most likely causes directly available based on serve state. Add expert shortcuts without adding novice clutter.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable distill`

### [P1] Lineup setup blocks keyboard users and makes deletion unsafe

**What it is:** Court slots and roster rows are clickable div elements without keyboard semantics. Edit and delete icons lack accessible names and shrink to 30×30 px on mobile. Player deletion happens at once.

**Why it matters:** A keyboard user cannot build a lineup. A courtside user can hit the wrong small target and permanently remove a player.

**Fix:** Use real buttons for players and court slots. Add accessible names. Enforce 44×44 px targets. Confirm deletion or provide an Undo toast that restores the player and lineup position.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable audit`

### [P2] Post-match Review reads like an internal prototype

**What it is:** Review describes rows as “Compact,” charts as “Clean,” displays render time and a 500 ms target, and History prints raw event types beside friendly descriptions.

**Why it matters:** The coach sees implementation language instead of match insight. The product's emotional ending feels unfinished.

**Fix:** Lead with final result, turning point, top performers, and useful patterns. Remove style and performance notes. Replace raw event types with coach-facing labels. Treat match completion as brief earned celebration.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

### [P2] Accessibility quality changes sharply between screens

**What it is:** The app has no reduced-motion rule. Login focus styles do not cover all custom buttons. Icon-only actions are not consistently labeled. Live measurement found 39 px authentication tabs, below the product's 44 px minimum.

**Why it matters:** Motion-sensitive, keyboard, screen-reader, and motor-access users receive a reliable experience on some screens and a weak one on others.

**Fix:** Add a global reduced-motion rule. Standardize `:focus-visible`. Label icon-only controls. Enforce the 44×44 px minimum across all breakpoints.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable audit`

## Persona Red Flags

### Jordan — first-timer

Home explains the first step well. Team & Lineup then presents team details, opponent, first serve, a six-slot court, player form, bench controls, roster list, and a sticky footer together. The position labels S, OH, MB, OPP, L, and DS are not defined. Blank player or team saves can silently do nothing. Jordan is likely to stall during roster creation.

### Casey — distracted mobile coach

Casey taps a player, scrolls below the court, and chooses from eight outcomes after the scoreboard and positions have left view. Game Review is absent from the phone toolbar. This creates a high risk of missed or incorrect entries during the product's primary task.

### Sam — accessibility-dependent user

Sam can sign in with labeled fields and receives an announced error. Sam then cannot focus the lineup's clickable roster rows or court slots, cannot identify unlabeled edit/delete icons reliably, and cannot request reduced motion. The main flow fails before Sam reaches Live Court, which has the app's strongest keyboard support.

## Minor Observations

- Sign-in has no password-recovery route.
- The sign-in CTA uses Volt even though the design rules reserve Volt for the wordmark and match-winning moments.
- History's empty state has no direct action to set up the first match.
- History labels its toolbar action “Live Court” even when the route guard will send the user to Team & Lineup.
- The wide History box score does not clearly signal horizontal scrolling.
- The final-match banner uses a warning symbol where the product brief calls for brief celebration.
